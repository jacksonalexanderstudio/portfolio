01

Layout

The bones of a page: regions, columns, edges, and how they hold when the width changes.

01.01

Viewport

also window, screen · visible area, what fits on screen, without scrolling, window size, screen size, the browser window

The area of the page visible in the browser window at one moment. The page itself can be far taller.

Not the page (which scrolls past it) or the container (which is narrower).

Let the hero fill the viewport and the next section start just inside it.

01.02

Container

also wrapper, content column, max-width · centered column, content width, how wide the content goes, max width, wrapper, the middle column

The centered column that holds content at a fixed maximum width, leaving the viewport’s sides empty on wide screens.

Not the viewport (the window) or a card (one contained unit).

Cap the container at 1200 and center it.

01.03

Page margin

also outer margin, side gutter · space at the edges, distance from the edge of the screen, side space, edge padding, off the glass

The space between the container’s edge and the viewport’s edge. At phone width it is the only thing keeping text off the glass.

Not the gutter (between columns) or padding (inside a box).

Hold a 24px page margin at phone width.

01.04

Column grid

also 12-column grid, grid system, columns · divide into columns, line things up, twelve columns, grid system, the underlying grid, column structure

The division of the container into equal vertical columns with gutters between, which every element aligns to. Twelve columns divide evenly by 2, 3, 4 and 6.

Not a card grid (a layout of items) or the baseline grid (vertical rhythm).

Put it on the 12-column grid: text spanning 7, image spanning 5.

01.05

Gutter

also column gap · space between columns, column spacing, gap between columns

The fixed space between two columns of the grid. Content never sits in it.

Not margin (outside the container) or padding (inside an element).

Widen the gutters to 32 so the columns stop touching.

01.06

Span

also column span · stretch across columns, take up half the width, how many columns wide, two thirds wide

The number of grid columns an element occupies. On twelve columns: halves are 6, thirds 4, quarters 3.

Not a width in pixels; a span is counted in columns.

Let the quote span 8 columns starting at column 3.

01.07

Row

also band · horizontal strip, row height, one line of the grid, band across

One horizontal band of the grid. Rows stack; the layout’s height is the sum of them.

Not a section (a semantic region that may hold many rows).

Give the ledger 56px rows.

01.08

Section

also band, block, strip · a part of the page, chunk of the page, full-width area, block of content, the next part down

A full-width horizontal region of the page with one purpose: hero, work grid, contact. Sections stack top to bottom.

Not a card or a row; a section usually holds several of each.

Add a section between the work grid and the footer for the shop.

01.09

Hero

also opener, lead · the big first thing, top of the page, opening image, first screen, the intro area, banner at the top

The first section, sized and typeset to carry the page’s thesis in one look.

Not the header (the thin identity strip above it). Not necessarily full-viewport.

Size the hero to its content, not to the viewport.

01.10

Header

also masthead, top bar, site header · the strip at the top, logo and links at the top, top bar, site header, the top of every page

The strip across the top of every page carrying identity and primary navigation.

Not the hero (the first content section) or a section heading.

Keep the header 56px and let it go transparent over the hero.

01.11

Nav

also navigation, menu · the links at the top, menu, links to other pages, navigation bar, the menu items

The set of links that move between pages or sections. May live in the header, a sidebar, or the footer.

Not the header itself; the header holds the nav.

Move the nav into a left sidebar, index style.

01.12

Sidebar

also rail, side column · column on the side, left panel, side navigation, the index on the left, side rail

A narrow vertical column beside the main content, holding navigation, an index, or notes.

Not an aside (a content block that can sit anywhere) or a drawer (which slides in).

Make the sidebar sticky so the index stays in view.

01.13

Footer

also page foot · the bottom of the page, contact at the bottom, bottom strip, the very end of the page

The strip at the bottom of every page: contact, colophon, secondary links.

Not a section; the footer repeats on every page.

Collapse the footer to one line at phone width.

01.14

Aside

also sidenote, marginal note, callout · note in the margin, side note next to the text, callout box, related info beside

A block of related but secondary content placed beside the main flow, often in the margin.

Not the sidebar (site furniture) or a pull quote (lifted from the text itself).

Put the material list in an aside beside the essay.

01.15

Fold

also above the fold, below the fold · what you see before scrolling, first screen, above the scroll, the cutoff of the first view

The bottom edge of the first viewport. What sits above it is seen without scrolling.

Not a fixed pixel height; the fold moves with the window.

Get the first three tiles above the fold.

01.16

Full-bleed

also edge-to-edge, bleed · touch the edges, no margin, all the way across, goes to the edge, wall to wall, wider than the content

An element that runs to the viewport edges, ignoring the container’s margins.

Not a wide container; a bleed has no margin at all.

Let the image go full-bleed but keep the caption in the container.

01.17

Inset

also set in, indented · pushed in from the edges, set inside, with space around it, indented box, not touching the edge

Positioned inward from its parent’s edges by a fixed distance on every side.

Not padding (space inside a box), though the result looks the same; inset describes placement.

Inset the video 24px inside the tile.

01.18

Offset

also shifted, nudged, staggered · move it down a bit, push it over, shifted, staggered, not lined up on purpose, nudged

Moved from its natural grid position by a deliberate distance, usually to break alignment on purpose.

Not misaligned (accidental) or inset (moved inward on every side).

Offset the second column down by one row.

01.19

Overlap

also layering, stacking, z-order · on top of, behind, in front of, layered over, stacked on top, sitting over the image

Elements sharing the same space, ordered front to back. Order is set by z-index inside a stacking context.

Not an overlay (a translucent layer over an image) or a modal (a layer over the whole page).

Let the title overlap the image’s bottom edge by half its height.

01.20

Sticky

also pinned on scroll · stays when I scroll, follows me, sticks to the top, stays in view, pinned while scrolling, locks at the top

Scrolls with the page until it reaches an edge, then holds there while its parent is in view.

Not fixed (never in the flow at all).

Make the section title sticky at the top while its grid scrolls past.

01.21

Fixed

also pinned, locked · always there, does not move when scrolling, stays put, locked to the screen, floating bar, pinned

Anchored to the viewport regardless of scrolling. A fixed bar never moves.

Not sticky (which starts in the flow).

Fix the nav to the bottom edge on phones.

01.22

Stack

also vertical stack, stacked · one under another, in a column, pile up vertically, on top of each other, stack on mobile

Items placed one under another in a single column with a set gap. The default when a row wraps at phone width.

Not a list (items of one kind) or overlap (shared space).

Stack the two panes at tablet width.

01.23

Split

also two-up, 50/50, side by side, three-up · half and half, side by side, next to each other, two panes, left and right, sixty forty

Two or more panes sharing a row. A 60/40 split leans one side; two-up and three-up name the count.

Not columns of one text; the panes hold different things.

Make it a 60/40 split with the image on the left.

01.24

Aspect ratio

also proportion, ratio · shape of the box, square, widescreen, portrait or landscape, sixteen by nine, proportions, tall or wide

Width to height of a box, written width:height. 1:1 square, 3:2 photographic, 16:9 video, 4:5 portrait.

Not size; boxes of any size can share a ratio.

Hold every tile at 3:2 and let the image crop to it.

01.25

Breakpoint

also media query · when it changes for phone, mobile version, tablet size, the width where it switches, responsive point

The viewport width at which the layout changes rules: columns drop, the sidebar stacks, type steps down.

Not a device; a breakpoint is a width.

Add a breakpoint at 860 where the sidebar stacks above the grid.

01.26

Fluid

also responsive, elastic · scales with the window, smoothly resizes, stretches to fit, grows with the screen, percentage width

Sizing that scales continuously with the viewport rather than stepping at breakpoints, usually in percentages or viewport units.

Not adaptive (which steps at breakpoints).

Make the hero type fluid between 32 and 72.

01.27

Masonry

also Pinterest grid · uneven grid, different heights packed, pinterest style, staggered tiles, no straight rows

A grid whose items keep their own heights and pack upward into columns, leaving no fixed rows.

Not a card grid (equal rows).

Not masonry; keep the rows even.

02

Type

Letters, lines, and the measurements between them.

02.01

Typeface vs font

also family, face, cut · the font, change the font, which typeface, font family, the type

A typeface is the design: Archivo. A font is one cut of it: Archivo Medium at 15px. Name a typeface; specify a font.

Not interchangeable in a brief; “change the font” may mean the weight or the family.

Keep the typeface, change the font: Medium instead of Regular.

02.02

Weight

also thin, light, regular, medium, semibold, bold, black · heavier, thicker, lighter type, darker text, bolder, too bold, too thin

The stroke thickness of a cut, on a scale from 100 (Thin) to 900 (Black). 400 is Regular, 700 is Bold.

Not size or tracking; a heavier weight at the same size darkens the block.

Drop the headings from 700 to 500.

02.03

Size

also point size, px · bigger text, smaller text, font size, point size, too small to read

The height of the em box, not of any letter. Two faces at 15px can read as different sizes because their x-heights differ.

Not cap height or x-height (the visible measures).

Bump the body to 16 and hold the leading at 24.

02.04

Leading

also line-height, line spacing · space between lines, lines too close, lines too far apart, cramped lines, line spacing, double spaced, open up the lines

The distance from one baseline to the next. Named for the strips of lead set between lines of metal type. Written as a ratio (1.5) or a length (24px).

Not tracking (between letters) or paragraph spacing (between blocks).

Open the body leading from 1.4 to 1.6.

02.05

Tracking

also letter-spacing · space between letters, spread the letters, letters too tight, loosen the letters, letter spacing, spaced out caps

Uniform space added between every letter of a run. Set in em (.04em) so it scales with the size.

Not kerning (one pair) or word spacing.

Track the caption out .04em and leave the body at zero.

02.06

Kerning

also pair spacing · gap between two letters, the A and V look far apart, two letters too close, uneven letter gaps, pair spacing

The adjustment of space between one specific pair of letters, such as AV or To, so their gap matches the rest. Fonts carry kerning tables; the browser applies them when font-kerning is on.

Not tracking, which applies to every pair.

The W and A are gapping in the wordmark; kern them in.

02.07

Measure

also line length, column width · lines too long, too many words per line, column too wide to read, line length, narrow the text

The length of a line of text, counted in characters. 45 to 75 reads comfortably; 66 is the classic target.

Not the container width; measure is counted in characters at the set size.

Cap the essay’s measure at 68 characters.

02.08

Baseline

also base line · the line the letters sit on, text not lining up, bottoms of the letters, align the text bottoms

The invisible line the letters sit on. Descenders hang below it. Baselines, not box edges, are what the eye aligns.

Not the bottom of the text box.

Align the caption’s baseline to the heading’s, not the boxes.

02.09

x-height / cap height

also x-height, cap height · how tall the lowercase is, height of capitals, looks small for its size, short letters

x-height is the height of a lowercase x; cap height is the height of an H. Together they set how large a face reads at a given size.

Not size; a large x-height reads bigger at the same px.

Pick a face with a taller x-height so 14px reads.

02.10

Ascender / descender

also ascender, descender · the tails of letters hanging down, the tall parts of letters, the g hangs below, letters touching the next line

Strokes rising above the x-height (b, d, k) and dropping below the baseline (g, p, y). Tight leading lets descenders touch the next line’s ascenders.

Not part of the cap height; ascenders often rise past it.

Not enough leading; the descenders are hitting the caps below.

02.11

Case

also uppercase, lowercase, sentence case, title case, all caps, small caps · capital letters, capitalize, all caps, lowercase everything, small capitals, first letter capital

Which letters are capitalized. Sentence case capitalizes the first word; Title Case Every Major Word; ALL CAPS everything; small caps use capital forms at x-height.

Not a font change; set it with text-transform.

Set the nav in sentence case, not title case.

02.12

Alignment

also flush left, ragged right, centered, justified, flush right, left-aligned · line up the text to the left, center the text, justify, even edges both sides, right aligned, ragged

How lines relate to an edge. Flush left with a ragged right is the reading default; justified squares both edges and can open rivers; centered is for short display lines.

Not the alignment of boxes on the grid; that is registration.

Flush-left the intro; no centering.

02.13

Rag

also ragged edge · uneven right edge, looks like stairs, jagged edge of the text, shape of the line ends

The uneven right edge of flush-left text. A good rag alternates long and short lines without making a shape; a bad rag makes stairs or a hole.

Not justification, which removes the rag.

The rag in the intro makes a staircase; rebreak it.

02.14

Widow / orphan

also widow, orphan · one word alone on the last line, single line at the top, lonely last word, stranded line

A widow is the last line of a paragraph alone at the top of a column; an orphan is a single word alone on the last line. Both are fixed by rebreaking or changing the measure.

Not a synonym pair; the widow is a line, the orphan a word.

Kill the orphan on the second paragraph.

02.15

Hierarchy

also type scale, levels · what should be read first, heading sizes, levels of importance, everything looks the same size, title subtitle body

The ranked sizes and weights that tell a reader what to read first: display, heading, subhead, body, caption. Three levels usually suffice.

Not decoration; every level must encode a real rank.

Flatten the hierarchy to three levels.

02.16

Display vs text

also headline face, reading face, optical size · big type versus small type, headline font, reading font, poster size, body size

Display sizes (above about 24px) carry personality and take tight tracking; text sizes (13 to 17px) need even color and open spacing. Some families ship separate optical cuts.

Not just big and small; the setting rules differ.

Use the display cut above 40 and the text cut below.

02.17

Eyebrow

also kicker, overline, superhead · the little label above the title, small caps line on top, category label above, tiny tracked label

A short line set above a headline, usually small, uppercase and tracked, naming a category or context.

Not a dek (below the headline). In this studio it counts as scaffolding in creative work.

Cut the eyebrows; let the headings self-introduce.

02.18

Dek

also subhead, standfirst, deck, subtitle · the line under the headline, subtitle, intro sentence under the title, summary line

The line beneath a headline that expands it in one sentence. Larger than body, lighter than the head.

Not an eyebrow (above) or a caption (under an image).

Add a one-sentence dek under the case study title.

02.19

Rule

also hairline, divider, separator, line · a line between things, thin line, divider line, underline between sections, horizontal line

A drawn line that separates or groups. A hairline is the thinnest rule that will render, usually 1px.

Not a border (which wraps a box) or a gap.

Replace the card borders with a single hairline above each.

02.20

Indent

also first-line indent, hanging indent, outdent · push the first line in, first line spacing, hanging bullet, tab in the paragraph

A first-line indent pushes a paragraph’s opening in; a hanging indent pushes every line but the first; an outdent pulls something past the margin.

Not padding.

Use a 1.5em first-line indent instead of paragraph spacing.

02.21

Figures

also numerals, lining, oldstyle, tabular, proportional · numbers that line up in columns, numerals, digits look uneven, old style numbers, table numbers

Lining figures stand cap height; oldstyle figures dip like lowercase. Tabular figures share one width so columns align; proportional figures fit naturally in prose.

Not “numbers” as quantities; this is their drawing.

Switch the ledger to tabular lining figures.

02.22

Optical alignment

also hanging punctuation, optical margin · the quote marks stick out, looks misaligned but is not, hang the punctuation, align by eye

Aligning by what the eye sees rather than the glyph box: quotation marks hang outside the margin, round letters overshoot the baseline.

Not a bug; the mathematically aligned version is what looks wrong.

Hang the opening quote outside the margin.

02.23

Classification

also grotesk, geometric, humanist, neo-grotesque, serif, slab, mono · what kind of font, type of typeface, sans versus serif, geometric, grotesque, humanist, monospace

Sans families by construction: grotesk (19th-century, slightly irregular: Archivo), geometric (circles and lines: Futura), humanist (pen-formed: Gill). Serifs by era: old-style, transitional, modern, slab.

Not brand names; a classification describes structure.

Swap the geometric for a grotesk; it is reading too soft.

02.24

Drop cap / pull quote

also drop cap, pull quote, initial · big first letter, a quote pulled out big, large opening letter, highlighted quote in the text

A drop cap is an enlarged opening letter set into the first lines; a pull quote is a line lifted from the text and set large beside it.

Not a heading.

Pull one line from the essay as a quote at 32px.

02.25

Running head / folio

also running head, folio, page furniture · the small text at the top of each page, page number, chapter name at the top, page header

The small line at the top of every page naming the section (running head) and the page number (folio). Print terms that carry into long web documents.

Not the header (site identity and nav).

Add a running head with the chapter title.

02.26

Hyphenation

also hyphens, word break · break words at the end of the line, hyphens, split the word, word breaks

Breaking a word across lines at a syllable to improve the rag or the justification. Off by default on the web.

Not a dash used as punctuation.

Turn on hyphenation for the narrow column.

03

Space

Distances, rhythms, and the grids that make them regular.

03.01

Padding

also inner spacing, inset spacing · space inside, room inside the box, breathing room inside the button, inner spacing, text touching the edge of the box

Space inside an element, between its edge and its content. Part of the element: a background fills it.

Not margin (outside the edge).

Give the cards 24px padding on all sides.

03.02

Margin

also outer spacing · space outside, push it away from the next thing, gap around, outer spacing, distance between blocks

Space outside an element, between its edge and its neighbours. Empty: no background fills it. Vertical margins can collapse into each other.

Not padding (inside).

Add 48px margin above each section heading.

03.03

Gap

also grid gap, spacing between items · space between the tiles, grid spacing, distance between items, tile spacing, column and row gaps

Space between siblings in a grid or flex row, set once on the parent instead of as margins on each child.

Not gutter (the grid-system term), though the two coincide in a column grid.

Set the tile grid gap to 32.

03.04

Whitespace

also negative space, empty space, air · empty area, blank space, negative space, air, room around things, nothing there on purpose

The unmarked area of a layout. Structural, not leftover: it groups, separates and ranks without a single line.

Not white; the ground can be any color.

More whitespace between sections; the page reads cramped.

03.05

Baseline grid

also 4px grid, 8px grid, vertical grid · lines that all the text sits on, vertical grid, text lines up across columns, four pixel grid, eight pixel grid

Horizontal lines at a fixed interval that every leading and block space is a multiple of, so text in neighbouring columns shares baselines.

Not the column grid (vertical divisions).

Everything on a 4px baseline grid; leadings at 20, 24 and 32.

03.06

Spacing scale

also spacing tokens, space system · set of spacing values, steps, consistent spacing, spacing system, the next size up

The fixed set of distances used everywhere: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64. No value outside the set.

Not “a little more”; every step is named.

Use the next step on the spacing scale: 32, not 28.

03.07

Modular scale

also type scale, ratio · sizes that grow by a ratio, type sizes, size steps, how the sizes relate, scale of sizes

Type sizes derived from one base by a ratio: 15 × 1.25 gives 15, 19, 23, 29, 37. The ratio, not taste, sets each step.

Not the spacing scale; this one governs size.

Derive the headings from a 1.333 scale off 15.

03.08

Vertical rhythm

also rhythm · even spacing down the page, consistent up and down, the beat of the page, regular spacing

The regular pulse of a page from top to bottom, produced when every leading and every block space sits on the baseline grid.

Not consistent margins alone; rhythm needs the grid.

The rhythm breaks at the ledger; its rows are off-grid.

03.09

Optical centering

also optical vs mathematical · looks off center even though it is centered, sits too low, visually centered, center by eye

Placing something slightly above true center, where the eye reads it as centered. Mathematically centered marks look low.

Not misalignment.

Optically center the mark in the tile; it is sitting low.

03.10

Density

also tight, loose, airy, dense, compact · too much packed in, too spread out, tight, airy, compact, loose, how much is on screen

How much content sits in a given area. Set by the spacing scale, not by shrinking type. Dense reads as effortful or lazy depending on order; airy reads as calm.

Not size; a dense layout can have large type.

Too dense; move every spacing value up one step.

03.11

Registration

also alignment edge, shared edge · they do not line up, line them up, share an edge, same left edge, align the edges

Several elements sharing one edge or baseline. What the grid exists to produce.

Not centering.

Register the caption’s left edge with the image’s.

03.12

Hang

also outdent, hanging · stick out into the margin, bullets outside the text, numbers in the margin, outdent

Letting an element extend past the alignment edge so the text it belongs to stays registered: a bullet, a quotation mark, an index number.

Not an offset (which breaks alignment on purpose).

Hang the index numbers in the margin.

03.13

Breathing room

also air, room · give it room, let it breathe, too tight, more air, claustrophobic, needs space

Spacing generous enough that elements read as separate objects at rest. In this studio the floors are 16px gaps, 18 to 24px card padding, and 36px above section heads.

Not whitespace as decoration; the floor is functional.

Let the form breathe; double the row height.

03.14

Proximity

also grouping · things near each other look related, group by closeness, label close to its field, which things belong together

Items closer to each other than to anything else read as one group. The cheapest way to group without lines or boxes.

Not a border.

Use proximity: pull the label toward its field, away from the previous one.

04

Color

Hue, value, contrast, and the layers that carry them.

04.01

Hue

also color family · what color it is, red or blue, the actual color, shift the color, color family

The color’s position on the wheel: red, orange, blue. Two colors can share a hue at different saturation and lightness.

Not “color” in general; hue is one of three dimensions.

Shift the accent’s hue warmer without changing its lightness.

04.02

Saturation

also chroma, vividness, intensity · too bright, too colorful, washed out, muted color, vivid, intense, dull color, grey it down

How far a color sits from grey at the same lightness. Full saturation is vivid; zero is grey.

Not lightness (light vs dark) or the brightness of a screen.

Desaturate the accent by 20%.

04.03

Lightness

also value, tone, luminance · lighter, darker, brightness, value, how light it is

How light or dark a color is, independent of hue. What black-and-white photography preserves.

Not saturation.

Keep the hue; lift the lightness to 70%.

04.04

Tint / shade / tone

also tint, shade, tone · lighter version, darker version of a color, pastel version, add white, add black, greyed version

A tint adds white to a hue, a shade adds black, a tone adds grey. Tints of the stone make the figure grounds here.

Not “a lighter version” loosely; say which.

Use a 24% tint of the stone for the ground.

04.05

Accent

also highlight color, brand color · the one color used for emphasis, highlight color, the brand color, the pop of color, link color

The single color used sparingly against the neutrals to mark emphasis, links and active states. Here it is warm stone, #8C8880.

Not a palette; one accent, spent rarely.

Only the active number takes the accent.

04.06

Neutral

also grey, warm grey, cool grey · grey tones, the greys, warm grey, cool grey, background greys

A low-saturation color used for grounds and text. A neutral with a slight hue bias reads as chosen; a pure grey reads as default.

Not black or white, which are the poles.

Warm the neutrals toward the stone.

04.07

Foreground / background

also fg/bg, ground, text color · text color and background color, what is on what, the ground, the color behind

Foreground is what is drawn (text, marks); background is what it is drawn on. Contrast is measured between the two.

Not front and back in stacking order.

Invert: stone foreground on ink background.

04.08

Surface

also layer ground, panel background · background of a card or panel, panel color, card background, layer color

The background of a distinct layer: page, card, sheet. Each surface can take its own tint to separate it from the one below.

Not the page background alone.

Give the drawer its own surface one step lighter than the page.

04.09

Contrast

also contrast ratio · hard to read, too faint, not enough difference, text disappears, legibility, stands out enough

The luminance difference between foreground and background, written as a ratio. 4.5:1 is the floor for body text; 3:1 for large text.

Not bold; contrast is about color, weight is about stroke.

The stone on white is 3.2:1; darken it to pass.

04.10

Opacity

also alpha, transparency, translucent · see-through, partly transparent, translucent, faded, half visible, transparency

How much of what lies beneath shows through, 0 (invisible) to 1 (solid). A translucent layer takes on the color behind it.

Not a tint; opacity changes with the background.

Drop the scrim to 40% opacity.

04.11

Muted

also knocked back, desaturated, subdued, quiet · less loud, calmer color, toned down, knocked back, quieter grey, subdued

Lowered in saturation or contrast so it reads as secondary. The stone-70 captions here are muted ink.

Not disabled (an interaction state), though the look is similar.

Mute the captions; they compete with the body.

04.12

Warm / cool

also warm, cool, temperature · yellowish grey, bluish grey, warmer, cooler, temperature of the color

Warm colors lean toward red and yellow, cool toward blue. Neutrals lean too: this stone is warm.

Not the color temperature of light in a photo, though the words match.

The grey reads cool against the photos; warm it.

04.13

Monochrome

also single hue, greyscale, black and white · one color only, black and white, greyscale, no color, single color

One hue in several lightnesses, or no hue at all. The figures in this index are monochrome.

Not duotone (two hues mapped to shadows and highlights).

Keep the case study monochrome until the hero.

04.14

Duotone

also two-color · image in two colors, two tone, two color photo

An image remapped to two colors: one for shadows, one for highlights. A print process before it was a filter.

Not a tint overlay.

Duotone the archive scans: ink and stone.

04.15

Gradient

also fade, ramp, blend · fades from one color to another, blend, color ramp, ombre, shading from dark to light

A continuous transition between two or more colors across a distance or angle. Reads as generic when decorative; useful as a scrim.

Not a shadow.

No gradient on the hero; solid ink.

04.16

Overlay / scrim

also wash, tint layer, scrim · darken the image so the text reads, tint over the photo, film over the image, dark layer

A translucent layer laid over an image so text on it stays legible. A scrim is a gradient overlay that fades to nothing.

Not opacity on the image itself.

A 30% ink overlay on the hero so the title reads.

04.17

Inverted

also dark mode, reversed out, knockout type · white text on black, dark mode, reversed, flip the colors, light on dark

07.01

Transition

also state change · smooth change on hover, animate between, ease from one to the other, fade the change

A 200ms transition on the underline, nothing on the color.

Movement described by keyframes over time, independent of state. Can loop.

Not a transition.

A looping animation on the mark, 6s, ease-in-out.

Not delay.

07.04

also wait, offset in time · wait before it starts, pause then move, starts late, hold before

The wait before a motion begins. Used for staggers and holds.

Not duration.

Not duration.

Ease-out on the entrance, linear on the loop.

07.06

A light spring on the drawer, well damped, no bounce.

07.07

The same motion applied to a sequence of elements with a small delay between each, so they arrive in order.

07.08

A defined state at a point in an animation; the browser fills the frames between.

Not a frame of video.

also stereo, mono, mid-side, width, pan, L/R · wider, the sides, mono compatible, left and right, narrow it

Channel layouts. Mid is what left and right share; side is their difference. A width control scales the side. Mono compatibility means the mix survives the sides being cancelled.

Not panning, which moves a source between channels.

Mono-compatible; width control on the wet path only.

11.35

Audio thread

also real-time safety, audio callback, lock-free, allocation · it glitches when I open the window, dropouts, the audio must never wait, background versus audio

The audio callback must never wait: no memory allocation, no locks, no file access. The UI and the engine exchange data through lock-free queues and atomics, and buffers are preallocated.

Not CPU load.

No allocations on the audio thread; use a lock-free FIFO to the UI.

11.36

CPU load

also DSP load, performance, CPU meter · it is heavy, uses too much processor, the meter spikes, efficient

How much of the available processing time the plug-in consumes per block. Measured at a stated sample rate and buffer size; oversampling and lookahead raise it.

Not latency.

Keep it under 3% at 48k with a 128 buffer.

11.37

Capture / take / bounce

also record, take, bounce, resample, freeze, grab · record what it is doing, save the result as audio, drag it into the DAW, print it

Recording the plug-in’s output to a file (a take), or rendering a track in place (bounce or freeze). A grab captures the incoming signal into the plug-in’s own buffer instead.

Not the host’s transport record.

REC captures the wet output to a 24-bit WAV; the take chip drags into the DAW.

11.38

Ring buffer

also rolling buffer, circular buffer, ring · always has the last minute, keeps overwriting, the rolling capture, last 60 seconds

A fixed-length buffer that overwrites its oldest samples, so it always holds the last N seconds of input. The basis of any “grab what just played” feature.

Not the sample buffer it feeds.

A rolling 60 s input ring so GRAB always has the last minute.

11.39

Validation

also auval, pluginval, host scan, plugin scan · Logic will not load it, failed the scan, passes the test, shows up in the DAW

Host-side checks a plug-in must pass before it loads: auval for Audio Units, pluginval for general behaviour. A failed validation means the host hides the plug-in.

Not the project’s own unit tests.

Run auval after every build; Logic will not load it otherwise.

11.40

Demo / license

also trial, license key, activation, copy protection, iLok · try before buy, unlock it, the demo goes silent, serial number

How a commercial plug-in limits use before purchase (a time limit, periodic silence, no saving) and unlocks after. iLok is a hardware or cloud license system; a key file or serial is the lighter option.

Not a preset lock.

Demo: 14 days, then a 2 s silence every minute; no iLok.

Holding an element fixed in the viewport for a set scroll distance while its animation plays, then releasing it. The pinned distance adds to the page height.

Not sticky, which pins without a timeline.

Pin the chapter card for 205% of the viewport, then release.

12.04

Scrub

also scrubbed, scroll-scrubbed, tied to scroll, scrub lag · reverses when I scroll back, moves exactly with the wheel, trails the scroll a little, smoothed scrub

Linking animation progress directly to scroll position, so scrolling back reverses it. A scrub value adds lag (0.6 s) so progress eases toward the scroll position instead of snapping to it.

Not scroll-triggered, which plays once.

Scrub .6 so it trails the wheel slightly.

12.05

Progress

also progress value, 0 to 1, normalized progress · how far through, percent of the way, the number that drives everything

Where a scroll animation is between its start (0) and its end (1). Everything else can be driven from it: a curtain opening, a numeral counting, a color snapping.

Not scroll position in pixels.

Drive the well height from raw pin progress, not the smoothed timeline.

12.06

Timeline

also sequence, master timeline · one after another in order, chain the animations, the whole sequence as one thing

A container that sequences tweens in time, each placed relative to the others or to labels. One timeline can be scrubbed, paused or reversed as a unit.

Not a single tween.

Put the numeral, title and collage on one timeline and scrub it.

12.07

Tween

also tween, from / to, fromTo · animate this property from here to there, one movement, the individual animation

One animation of one or more properties from a start value to an end value over a duration with an ease. Timelines are made of tweens.

Not a keyframe, which is a single point.

A fromTo tween on the title: y 40 to 0, opacity 0 to 1.

12.08

Label / position

also label, position parameter, offset, <, +=0.2 · at the same time as, slightly after the last one, overlap them, start it here

Named points on a timeline that tweens can start from; a position parameter places a tween relative to the previous one: “<” with it, “+=0.2” after it, “-=0.1” overlapping it.

Not a delay on one tween.

Start the collage at the second-word label, not at the end.

12.09

Beat

also beat, hit, moment · land on the beat, the moment it snaps, when the second word arrives, key it to

A named moment in a sequence that other movements key to: the numeral snaps orange on the second-word beat. A choreography term, not a code one.

Not a keyframe, which is a value.

Collage and era-sub arrive on the final word’s beat.

12.10

Dead-hold

also hold, freeze, tail hold · stays still at the end of the pin, nothing moves for a while, let it sit before it scrolls away

A stretch of pinned scroll where nothing animates, so the finished state can be read before release. Usually the last 20 to 40% of a pin.

Not delay, which comes before.

Dead-hold the last 30% of the pin.

12.11

Lead-in

also lead, quiet start, pre-roll · a pause before anything happens, let the eye settle first, do not start immediately

The quiet time before the first movement, so the eye settles on the ground before anything arrives.

Not delay on a single tween; the lead-in is the whole sequence waiting.

Lead-in 0.4 s, then the first letter.

12.12

Release

also unpin, let go, release point · when it scrolls away again, the section lets go, back to normal scrolling

The moment a pinned element returns to the flow and scrolls away. Anything still mid-motion at release reads as broken.

Not the end trigger of an animation that never pinned.

Release the pin only after the dead-hold.

12.13

Toggle actions

also toggleActions, play once, reverse on leave, replay · play once and stay, replay every time it comes back, reverse when I scroll back up, what happens on the way out

What a triggered animation does on the four events: enter, leave, enter back, leave back. “Play, none, none, none” plays once and stays; “play, reverse, play, reverse” mirrors the scroll.

Not scrub.

Play once on enter and stay; no reverse on scroll-up.

12.14

Refresh

also recalculate, recalc, lateRefresh, re-measure · it breaks after the fonts load, wrong positions until I resize, measure again, the pin is off by a bit

Recomputing every trigger’s start and end after the layout changes: fonts loading, images arriving, a resize. Without it, positions were measured against the wrong page.

Not a page reload.

Refresh the triggers at fonts.ready; the first visit measures fallback type.

12.15

How fast the page is moving, used to drive effects that answer scrolling energy: skew, blur, marquee speed. Clamp it or it explodes on a flick.

Not momentum, which is the deceleration after release.

Skew the tiles by scroll velocity, clamped at 6 degrees.

Replacing the browser’s stepped wheel scrolling with an interpolated, eased position. Adds feel; can break native behaviour, anchors and accessibility.
