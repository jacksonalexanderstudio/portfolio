CASE STUDY 01: studio | BOSE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
Brand collateral, typography, and visual system design for a contemporary architectural studio.

Client: studio | BOSE
Discipline: Brand Collateral, Typography, Layout
Year: 2025

THE BRIEF:
With the logo already established, studio | BOSE needed the full collateral system built around it — proposals, invoices, typography, color palette, and layout templates. The goal was to create a cohesive visual language that extended the existing mark into every client-facing touchpoint, grounded in clean, architectural forms that reflected the studio's creative ethos.

TAKEAWAY:
The collateral system gave studio | BOSE a polished, consistent presence beyond the logo — every proposal, invoice, and document now carries the same intentional visual language. The work turned a single mark into a complete brand experience across every client touchpoint.

CASE STUDY 02: ROCKET RAMPS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
Custom illustration, brand collateral, and event merch for the grand opening of the Picuris Pueblo All-Wheel Park — northern New Mexico's newest bike park.

Client: Rocket Ramps
Discipline: Illustration, Brand Collateral, Merch
Year: 2025

THE BRIEF:
Rocket Ramps — a Santa Fe-based trail building company — needed collateral and merch for the grand opening of the Picuris Pueblo All-Wheel Park, a $500K bike park funded by the New Mexico Outdoor Recreation Division. Working within the existing RR brand mark, the goal was to build out a hand-drawn icon library, event apparel, and branded materials that captured the energy of the launch — a Best Whip and Trick competition with $8,000 in prizes and the community behind it.

PROCESS CAPTIONS:
- Icon studies, sprocket variations, and circular compositions
- Merch studies — icon-filled t-shirt silhouettes, light and dark
- More icon explorations — characters, objects, and creatures

TAKEAWAY:
The Picuris Pueblo All-Wheel Park opened July 19, 2025 to a packed crowd. What started as a collateral project became a full visual language — over a hundred hand-drawn icons that told the story of the land, the tools, and the people building on it. The kind of work where illustration and brand become the same thing. Huge thanks to Henry and Ted for bringing me on board for this project.

CASE STUDY 03: ISRU RITUAL
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
A short film documenting daily garden practice as ritual — produced as the final challenge for the Tom Sachs ISRU summer camp.

Project: Tom Sachs ISRU Summer Camp
Discipline: Film, Gardening, Production
Year: 2025

THE BRIEF:
The Tom Sachs ISRU summer camp was a three-month daily practice — structured around discipline, craft, and showing up every day. The final challenge was called Find Your Ritual. The garden became that ritual. Every morning, the same quiet work: watering, weeding, turning soil, tending to what's growing. This film is a brief glimpse into those daily tasks — the repetition, the pace, and the slow reward of paying attention to a piece of land all summer long. Photography by McCall Sides.

TAKEAWAY:
The garden was never about content or a final product — it was about doing the same thing every day and letting the work compound. Three months of showing up taught me more about discipline than any deadline ever has. The film was just the proof that it happened.

CASE STUDY 05: JERICHO RIDGE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
A hand-built dry-stacked stone structure on a mountain ridge outside Santa Fe — raw materials, simple tools, and a long view.

Project: Jericho Ridge
Discipline: Stone Fabrication, Land Work
Year: 2025

THE BRIEF:
A ridgeline at 8,000 feet with a 360-degree view of the Sangre de Cristos and the Rio Grande valley. The project was simple in concept — build a dry-stacked stone wind shelter using only the material already on the mountain. No mortar, no concrete, no power tools. Just a pickaxe, hands, and the rocks underfoot. The kind of work where you learn the land by moving it.

PROCESS CAPTIONS:
- Wall detail — dry-stacked stone with salvaged timber
- Terrace taking shape — pickaxe and hand-placed stone
- Walls rising — last light on the ridge
- Fall on the ridge — wall and autumn color
- Tools of the trade — rake and pickaxe on the wall face

TAKEAWAY:
Jericho Ridge started as an excuse to get off the computer. No client, no deadline — just hauling rocks up a hill and stacking them until they held. It turned into the thing I looked forward to most. Quiet work, outside, building something heavy and permanent with my hands. A good break from screens and a good way to stay connected to the land up here.

CASE STUDY 06: ZOZOBRA: 100 YEARS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
Poster design for Zozobra: 100 Years of Fire and Redemption — a documentary celebrating a century of Santa Fe's most iconic tradition.

Client: Hutton Films
Discipline: Poster Design, Film Branding
Year: 2024

THE BRIEF:
Every September, Santa Fe burns a 50-foot marionette called Zozobra — a tradition started by artist Will Shuster in 1924. For the centennial, Hutton Films produced a short documentary and needed a poster that captured the weight and weirdness of a hundred years of burning gloom. The ask was a single image that could work on screen, in print, and on the marquee at Violet Crown Cinema for the premiere.

PROCESS CAPTIONS:
- Archival reference — early Zozobra, Will Shuster era
- Modern Zozobra — fireworks and fire at Fiestas de Santa Fe

TAKEAWAY:
The poster ran on screen and in print across Santa Fe for the centennial premiere at Violet Crown Cinema. The black-and-white treatment grounded a hundred years of spectacle into something that felt archival and permanent — a document as much as a design. Sometimes the best work is one image, one line, and knowing when to stop.

CASE STUDY 07: 1974 BMW 2002tii
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
Bought a rough 1974 BMW 2002tii and brought it back to life — engine, wiring, suspension, bodywork, all of it. Learned most of it along the way.

Client: Personal
Discipline: Restoration, Fabrication, Mechanical
Year: 2021 – Present

THE BRIEF:
A 1974 BMW 2002tii — mechanically tired, electrically unreliable, and cosmetically faded after fifty years. The goal was never a concours trailer queen. It was to bring the car back to life as a real driver: rebuild the engine, rewire the dash, sort the suspension, and restore the details that make the 2002 one of the most important driver's cars ever made. All work done by hand, on nights and weekends, out of a Santa Fe garage.

PROCESS CAPTIONS:
- Engine tear-down — head pulled, block exposed, full top-end rebuild
- Reassembled — M10 back together, Original BMW Teile
- Before — fifty years of UV on original Verona Red
- After — cut, polish, and seal on the original paint
- Finished console — VDO gauges, Blaupunkt Frankfurt, BMW shift knob
- Restored interior — black sport seats, new floor mats, full cabin detail

TAKEAWAY:
My dad passed this car down to me. He kept it alive for years and I wanted to keep that going. It's been frustrating, expensive, and slow — most of the work I learned as I went, breaking things and fixing them again. But every problem taught me something, and now I know this car inside and out. It runs, it drives, and it's still in the family.

CASE STUDY 08: CHEYENNE TURQUOISE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
A short documentary following turquoise miner Jess Robbins into the Nevada desert — produced for Peyote Bird and the Sundance Catalog.

Client: Peyote Bird / Sundance Catalog
Discipline: Film Production, Documentary
Year: 2019

THE BRIEF:
Jess Robbins mines turquoise by hand from his claim deep in the desert outside Tonopah, Nevada. The raw stone goes into ingot-based jewelry he sells all over the world. Peyote Bird and the Sundance Catalog wanted a short documentary that told his story — the land, the process, and the person behind the material. I produced the film over a three-day shoot at the claim. Photography by Leroy Graffe.

TAKEAWAY:
Three days in the middle of nowhere with a small crew and a guy who's been pulling stone out of the ground for decades. No studio, no controlled environment — just desert, dust, and the real thing. The film captured something you can't fake: the quiet obsession of a person who knows exactly what they're looking for and where to find it. One of the most memorable shoots I've been part of.

CASE STUDY 09: ALL EYES ON US
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
A hand-made mixed-media zine exploring surveillance, privacy, and the architecture of observation — built from newspaper clippings, satellite imagery, redacted documents, and collage.

Client: Academic
Discipline: Editorial, Collage, Typography
Year: 2017

THE BRIEF:
Create a self-published zine as a final project for a college typography and graphic design course. The only constraint was format — everything else was open. The result was a 13-page hand-assembled exploration of mass surveillance, data collection, and the feeling of being watched. Every page was built physically: cut, torn, taped, redacted, and scanned. No templates, no digital illustration — just source material, an X-Acto knife, and a flatbed scanner.

TAKEAWAY:
The zine became the project where design thinking and personal point-of-view first merged. Building every page by hand — sourcing material from newspapers, redacting text, punching holes, layering tape — forced a slower, more deliberate process than any screen-based workflow. It was the first time the work felt like it had something to say beyond the assignment.

Collaboration with Ian McMullen

CASE STUDY 10: FLASH SHEETS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Tagline:
An ongoing library of original characters, glyphs, and symbols — hand-drawn in ink and organized into flash sheets for tattoo, print, and personal archive.

Project: Flash Sheets
Discipline: Illustration, Icon Design, Ink Drawing
Year: 2023 – Present

THE BRIEF:
No client, no deadline — just a growing collection of characters that started filling up sketchbooks and never stopped. Faces, figures, totems, creatures, glyphs, geometric forms — all drawn by hand in ink, then scanned and arranged into flash sheets. Some pull from tribal and indigenous visual language, some from tattoo tradition, some from somewhere harder to name. The goal was never a single finished piece — it was to build a library. A personal iconography that could live on skin, on paper, on screen, or on a wall.

TAKEAWAY:
The flash sheets became the throughline across everything else — the visual DNA that shows up in logo work, in the Rocket Ramps icons, in fabrication details, in things that haven't been made yet. Drawing every day without a brief turned out to be the most useful practice of all. The library keeps growing.
